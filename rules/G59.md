# G59 — The hand-off is written to be replayed, and the record keeps the why
id: G59 · group: G · status: active · tags: decomp,publishing,record · origin: decomp-architect · added: 2026-09-30

Every hand-off — a task summary, a `TASK_PROGRESS.md` written at a context threshold — is self-sufficient and verbose:
paths, commands, hashes, the exact next step; the next agent reads it whole and never works from a summary of it. A "clean"
close quotes the fleet's green count; tree-clean and fleet-green are different invariants. At a phase close, the closer's
recap and the phase's decision-log entry say what the phase believed, what failed and why it looked right, what it cost
(with its denominator) and what would have found it sooner: the retrospective is rebuilt from exactly that, and a terse
"what changed" record survives a context boundary while the why does not. A finding that lives only in a commit message is
lost — the commit log is not in the load order: before a hand-off or a close, idioms go to the cookbook, tool changes to
their ops rows, pivots to the decision log, and a late discovery that would have sped earlier work to `docs/accelerators.md`
the same session (what it is, when it was found, when it could have been, what it would have saved).
